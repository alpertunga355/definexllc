02

Rejection rules, enforced in code

Amazon rejects an upload for reasons that are entirely mechanical. So we made them mechanical on our side first.

  • Fonts embedded and subsetted — no standard-14 fallback, the single most common KDP rejection.
  • Gutter margin scaled to page count bracket, so body text never disappears into the binding.
  • Spine width computed from the final page count, not estimated.
  • Interiors generated to trim plus 0.125" bleed on every edge.
  • Covers laid out so title and author never sit on artwork.

03

Why this matters commercially

A rejected upload is not just a delay — it is a re-render, a re-check and a lost launch window. Encoding the rules once turns a recurring manual review into an assertion that either passes or stops the build. The marginal cost of the fifteenth title is materially lower than the first.

Same discipline, different output

This is the publishing instance of the pattern that runs through every DefineX system: put the constraint in code, assert it on every build, and let the generator be boring.
